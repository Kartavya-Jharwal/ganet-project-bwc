Executive summary. Team BWC managed a $1M paper book for Professor Daniela Neiderer's Investment Challenge (Feb 2 - Apr 11, 2026). We closed at -4.37% versus SPY -2.29%. The headline return is honest; the teaching point is whether the operating system held under stress.

Course expectations (CHL-0200): defend strategy and implementation math, maintain a bi-weekly trading diary, respect IPS limits (10% max single name), and submit a final committee report. Graded on strategy (60 pts), implementation (60 pts), diary (30 pts), and leadership via peer evaluation.

Team of eight: one unified desk spanning macro, risk, sectors, execution, factors, behavioral audit, and diary compliance.

About this page: Written and built by Kartavya Jharwal, Team BWC lead and quant architect. Faculty feedback and team links are at the end.

We operated a unified desk where every trade was documented against a specific macro rationale, ensuring that our technical analysis and quant models spoke the same language.

Our strategy was defined by high regime awareness, specifically our April 8 pivot following the US-Iran ceasefire announcement, which necessitated an immediate move from crisis hedges back into pro-cyclical recovery assets.

B / Performance vs SPY

FROM FINAL REPORT / EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Team 5 underperformed SPY by 208 bps, closing at $956,279.80 on $1,000,000 with Jensen’s alpha -0.000443 and information ratio -2.0. Risk architecture was defensible - beta ~0.63, peak-to-trough drawdown ~6.3% vs a deeper benchmark path in March - but closed-trade economics were negative (profit factor 0.17, win rate 28.6%, expectancy -$4,559 per trade). The failure mode was not the hedge in the shock; it was slow re-risking after the 8 Apr ceasefire while SPY recovered faster.

3 / CONFIDENCE VALIDATOR

Closed quant model - not a stock picker

We built a closed quantitative model as an additional confidence validator to fight confirmation bias and check our own decisions. It was never meant to fire buy/sell signals. It stress-tests each human thesis against historical factor loadings and regime labels so we can see accidental beta, sector, or correlation risk the IPS did not allow.

In a short classroom simulation, high-turnover gambling often wins on luck. We aimed for an institutional-style process built for long-horizon capital preservation: IPS limits, logged overrides, walk-forward tests, and attribution you can audit. The desk still lost -4.37% - the model helps explain why, not excuse it.

Pipeline in one line: DuckDB price matrix, journal NAV rebuild, sparse covariance, HRP weights, HMM regimes, Brinson-Fachler attribution, Cornish-Fisher VaR and jump Monte Carlo. Charts hydrate from results.json at freeze. Full package map for developers is in Section 4 (Stack).

How the validator is built

Three layers, fixed order: (1) what the desk did - Brinson-Fachler on realized weights; (2) tail risk on the overlay path - Cornish-Fisher VaR, jump-augmented MC; (3) what weights the regime model would have held - HMM to softmax over signal history, HRP on GraphicalLasso Σ.

HMM posterior sets regime; sleeve weights are a softmax over trailing signal P&L by regime - no hard 0/1 sector rotation. Used to flag when desk trades contradicted the labeled environment.

Covariance & simulation

Σ estimated via GraphicalLassoCV, linked through MST, weights from HRP. Forward paths: GBM + compound Poisson jumps; report 5% CF-VaR, not Gaussian VaR.

MST Dendrogram
$$dS_t = \mu S_t \, dt + \sigma S_t \, dW_t + S_t \, dJ_t$$

Jump term dJ_t captures gap risk absent in plain GBM - relevant for the March-April window in the simulation.

4 / FULL STACK MAP

For engineers and quant developers

Everything below lives in quant_monitor/ at the repo root. This microsite is the frozen public layer; the Python engine is the implementation reference.

DISCLAIMER

The mathematics here (sparse inverse covariance, factor regressions, hierarchical risk parity, mean-variance optimization) is standard portfolio theory, widely implemented in open source. We did not invent new finance. We wired known methods into a layered read so the desk can answer one question at a time: how did we perform, where did risk concentrate, and did our trades match the labeled regime. The goal is clarity, not jargon.

What the quant layer actually computes

  • GraphicalLassoCV sparse inverse covariance on the return matrix, then MST linkage for stable clusters.
  • Fama-French / Carhart / q-factor OLS on portfolio returns to see accidental factor bets.
  • HRP (hrp_sizer.py) weights from the estimated covariance graph.
  • Black-Litterman + MVO (optimizer.py) policy weights vs desk weights for gap analysis.
  • Brinson-Fachler attribution of allocation vs selection vs the trading log.
  • Cornish-Fisher VaR and jump-augmented Monte Carlo on the overlay path (not Gaussian VaR alone).

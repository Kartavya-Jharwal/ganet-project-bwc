B · Performance vs SPY

FROM FINAL REPORT · EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Team 5 underperformed SPY by 208 bps, closing at $956,279.80 on $1,000,000 with Jensen’s alpha −0.000443 and information ratio −2.0. Risk architecture was defensible — beta ~0.63, peak-to-trough drawdown ~6.3% vs a deeper benchmark path in March — but closed-trade economics were negative (profit factor 0.17, win rate 28.6%, expectancy −$4,559 per trade). The failure mode was not the hedge in the shock; it was slow re-risking after the 8 Apr ceasefire while SPY recovered faster.

4 · CONFIDENCE VALIDATOR

Closed quant model — not a stock picker

We built a closed quantitative model as an additional confidence validator to fight confirmation bias and check our own decisions. It was never meant to fire buy/sell signals. It stress-tests each human thesis against historical factor loadings and regime labels so we can see accidental beta, sector, or correlation risk the IPS did not allow.

In a short classroom simulation, high-turnover gambling often wins on luck. We aimed for an institutional-style process built for long-horizon capital preservation: IPS limits, logged overrides, walk-forward tests, and attribution you can audit. The desk still lost −4.37% — the model helps explain why, not excuse it.

Stack (from quant_monitor/ and engineering docs): DuckDB price matrix → journal NAV reconstruction → GraphicalLasso covariance → HRP weights → HMM regime labels → Brinson–Fachler attribution vs the trading log → Cornish–Fisher VaR and jump Monte Carlo on the overlay path. Post-simulation audit & macro rectifications

1 · Macro briefing context

Review: Our mid-semester rebalancing thesis leaned on expected stabilization in global energy volatility. Early drafts assumed the reader already knew the timeline.

Rectification: Reports now spell out the mechanism — the US–Iran ceasefire effective 8 April 2026, reopening of the Strait of Hormuz to commercial tankers, ~15% drop in Brent, and the follow-on equity rally we were slow to capture.

2 · Process vs headline return

Review: Over ~60 trading days, hyper-concentrated beta bets often top the leaderboard.

Reality: We ran under fiduciary-style limits — including a 10% max single-name weight — to cut idiosyncratic lottery risk. We finished down; the point is whether the process is repeatable under real capital.

3 · Why the Python model is not a signal engine

Friction: Several teammates wanted the model to spit out buy/sell alerts. We kept it a closed analytical validator instead — human team owns alpha generation; the engine owns risk validation. That separation is how you avoid confirmation bias in a real asset-management shop.

CORE TAKEAWAY

Knowing market theory is not the same as executing live trades under a deadline and stress. Strong teams need clear ownership, logged accountability, and fast coordination when the macro picture flips — not just attendance. This simulation was too short to prove long-term skill; it was long enough to test whether our controls, diaries, and validator would survive a real governance review.

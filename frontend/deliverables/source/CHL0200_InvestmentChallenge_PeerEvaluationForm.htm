Please reflect on your overall experience with the Challenge (what you learned, what it made you think about).

 

This challenge started with a simple belief: green is good, red is bad, buy cheap and sell high. I came in with some econ, finance, behavioural economics, game theory, and macroeconomics in the background, so I understood the broad logic of markets, but the first simulation made it clear that knowing the theory is very different from actually trading it.

 

What surprised me most was how messy the market is once real decisions, timing, and emotions enter the picture. The semester pushed me deeper into the theory and the course materials paired with the banking society, investors society, and AI with the quant python model, and that wider exposure helped me understand that finance is not just about reading charts or memorising terms. It is technical analysis, macro judgement, risk management, and execution and more all at once, and the simulation forced me to see that in a much more serious way.

 

The biggest lesson was that I did not know what I did not know. I started by knowing there was more to learn, but by the end I knew more clearly what I already know, what I need to learn next, and what kinds of questions actually matter. Moving forward, I want to treat this not just as a challenge, but as a personal finance and learning project: something practical, translatable, and useful beyond finance itself.

 

As team leader, I also learned that leading a strong group is not about pretending to do everything yourself. It is about coordinating people who are stronger than you in different areas and keeping the whole machine moving in the same direction. That became harder than expected because I had to be more authoritative, enforce a grand vision standard, and make sure the final deliverables matched the level of professionalism I personally wanted.
